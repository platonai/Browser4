## A. Task Result — Top 3 Hacker News Story Summaries

### Story #1: "Our position on open-weights models" (anthropic.com)
**335 points, 426 comments — by Dario Amodei (Anthropic CEO)**

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei clarifies the company's stance on open-weights AI models, explicitly stating they have **never advocated for a ban**. Key positions:
- Open-weights models without dangerous capabilities are a **public good**
- He identifies two nightmare scenarios: (1) authoritarian regimes achieving AI superiority for military/repression purposes, and (2) misuse for cyber/bio attacks
- He supports three specific measures instead of bans: **(a)** restrict chip sales to China, **(b)** crack down on industrial-scale distillation, and **(c)** require mandatory safety testing for all sufficiently capable models, open or closed
- He disagrees with the recent industry open letter's claim that open weights necessarily help defenders more than attackers — believes this should be empirically tested, not assumed

### Story #3: "Benchmarking Opus 5 on SlopCodeBench" (github.com/humanlayer)
**49 points, 10 comments — by dexhorthy**

An independent benchmark of Claude Opus 5 against SlopCodeBench, a long-horizon coding benchmark where models must evolve a codebase across multiple checkpoints without knowing future requirements. Key findings:
- **Opus 5 scored 24% strict pass rate** (4/17 checkpoints) vs. Opus 4.6's 17% in the original paper — still far from reliable
- All models showed **significant code quality degradation** over time: increasing complexity, verbosity, and duplication across checkpoints
- Opus 5 wrote **5x more functions** than Opus 4.8, and 93% of code lines triggered the benchmark's "slop" detectors
- Author concludes: for real-world iterative software engineering, today's models **cannot be relied on to run unattended** without human steering
- Proposes using frontier models to build code, then testing whether smaller models can extend it — as a signal of codebase maintainability
